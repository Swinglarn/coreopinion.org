Politics in Canada: the landscape behind this test

Canada is a parliamentary federation using first-past-the-post in single-member ridings. Governments are frequently minorities that survive through case-by-case agreements with smaller parties, which gives those parties real leverage without requiring formal coalitions.

The party system

The Liberal Party has governed for more of Canada's history than any other and has traditionally occupied a flexible centre. The Conservative Party, formed from the 2003 merger of the Progressive Conservatives and the Canadian Alliance, holds the centre-right. The New Democratic Party sits to their left and has repeatedly pushed governing parties toward social programmes, most consequentially on public healthcare.

Two parties have no equivalent elsewhere. The Bloc Quebecois runs candidates only in Quebec and advocates for Quebec's interests and sovereignty. The Green Party competes nationally on environmental grounds. Together they make Canadian Parliaments more fragmented than the electoral system alone would suggest.

The cleavages that actually divide voters

Region matters as much as ideology. The Prairie provinces, with economies tied to oil and gas, have grievances about federal energy and equalisation policy that do not map onto a simple left-right line. Quebec's distinct language, civil law tradition, and approach to secularism produce arguments that barely register elsewhere in the country. Ontario's population size means national campaigns are often fought there.

Housing affordability, immigration levels, healthcare capacity, and the balance between resource extraction and climate commitments are the issues that recur across recent campaigns.

How this maps onto the compass

Canadian politics sits left of the United States on economics, with universal public healthcare as settled consensus, while remaining more market-oriented than Nordic social democracy. On social questions the country is broadly permissive, and issues that remain contested south of the border are largely closed here.

This test asks about Canadian policy specifically, including federal-provincial questions that have no American analogue. For the full profiles, see the Canadian party guides.
