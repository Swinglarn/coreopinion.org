Politics in Ireland: the landscape behind this test

Ireland uses the single transferable vote in multi-member constituencies, a proportional system in which voters rank candidates. It produces close correspondence between votes and seats, rewards candidates with broad second-preference appeal, and creates competition between candidates of the same party.

A party system defined by history, then by housing

Fianna Fail and Fine Gael both trace their origins to opposing sides of the 1922 civil war rather than to a left-right economic split, which is why Irish politics long lacked the class-based alignment common elsewhere in Europe. Both occupy broadly centrist ground, and in 2020 they entered government together for the first time.

Sinn Fein has grown into a major force, campaigning on housing, health, and Irish unification, and its rise is the clearest sign that the civil war cleavage has finally given way to something closer to a conventional left-right contest. Labour, the Social Democrats, the Greens, and a large group of independents complete the field, with independents winning more seats than in almost any comparable democracy.

The cleavages that actually divide voters

Housing dominates. Sustained shortages and among the highest rents and prices in Europe have made it the defining domestic issue and the principal driver of electoral change. Health service capacity and waiting lists follow closely.

Ireland transformed socially within a generation, legalising same-sex marriage by popular vote in 2015 and liberalising abortion by referendum in 2018, both by decisive margins in a country where the Catholic Church once held enormous authority. Northern Ireland, the border, and the prospect of unification remain live constitutional questions. Corporate tax policy is unusually consequential given the role of multinationals in the economy.

How this maps onto the compass

Ireland combines a low corporate tax rate and a very open economy with public healthcare that is less comprehensive than most of Western Europe, leaving significant private provision. It sits closer to the economic centre than most EU members. Socially it has moved rapidly toward permissiveness.
